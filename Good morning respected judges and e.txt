Good morning respected judges and everyone here. We are Team Sherpas, and today we’re excited to present our app called MAARG. 

We all know that organizations like NATPAC in Kerala need accurate travel data from citizens But today, this data is collected mostly through manual surveys, which are slow, time-consuming, and cover only a small number of people.
This is where MAARG helps. Our app automatically collects trip data like origin, destination, mode of travel, timing, and stay duration—directly from the users’ mobile phones. With user consent, this data is securely sent to a central database, where NATPAC scientists can analyze it.

Now let’s talk about the feasibility of our idea.
First, our app is technically feasible because we are using Flutter, Firebase, and Google APIs – these are standard, well-documented technologies.
Second, Kerala has high smartphone usage, so we can reach a wide user base easily.
Third, we can add a reward system where users earn points that can connect with government portals and services.
Also, the Kerala government’s digital governance initiatives make it easier to integrate such systems.
Finally, our cloud infrastructure ensures scalability – it can handle thousands of users at the same time.

Of course, there are some challenges.
Now, let me quickly talk about some challenges we might face and how we plan to handle them.
Continuous GPS tracking may drain the battery. Users might have privacy concerns when sharing data. And in dense urban areas, GPS can sometimes give inaccurate results.
But we also have strategies to overcome them. For example, we’ll use smart tracking so GPS is used only when needed. To keep users engaged, we’ll add gamification like rewards and badges. And finally, by combining multiple data sources like GPS, WiFi, and cell towers, we can improve accuracy.
